# LevelMeUp — App audit

**Date:** 2026-10-15 · **Version audited:** 0.5.0 on `main` (`4f3f55c`) · **Asked by:** the owner ("audit the app: how useful and
valuable it is, potential, strengths, weaknesses, and what we can do to complete the final version, except the Play
Store for now").

**Status (2026-10-15):** the code is backed up in the private GitHub repo `dannytist73/LevelMeUp`
(decision 1, the owner's yes). Phase 0's fixes 0.2-0.6 are done at the owner's yes: merged into `main`, the functions
deployed (`lmu-sync` v20, the live suite 50 of 50), and the 0.5.1 preview APK built (EAS build `494daa00`). CI (0.1)
and crash reports leaving the phone wait for a decision. Next: Phase 1, the real pilot.

**How it was done:** three read-only reviews run side by side (product against the specs and the roadmap, engineering
against the code, and the market on the web), my own use of the app on the emulator, and the live pilot report
(`npm run pilot`, read only). Claims that couldn't be checked are marked *unverified*. Nothing was changed, deployed or
spent.

## Plain summary

**The verdict:** LevelMeUp is a well-made, safe, unusually kind take on the "Solo Leveling System" idea. It is built
far more carefully than anything else in its niche, which is mostly ad-heavy apps from solo developers that lose
players' progress. **But nobody has really used it yet.** The pilot has 3 characters, one of them the emulator's test
character, and most of the real-phone checks are blank. So today the app's value is a strong guess, not a fact.

**What makes it valuable:**

- It turns "I should exercise, read and focus more" into three or four small, safe quests a day, sized to the
  player's minutes, equipment and limits.
- Missing a day doesn't punish the player. It asks what happened and helps them back.
- It has a look and a voice of its own.

**What holds it back:**

1. **Nothing to grow into after the first month.** The last ability and the last portrait arrive at level 10, about
   three weeks in. Most ladders top out after five to eight weeks.
2. **The daily habit has no reminder.** Notifications are off until the player finds them in Settings.
3. **Rigid and solo.** Players can't add their own habits, can't play with friends, and can't change their goals or
   limits after onboarding.
4. **The engineering safety nets are missing.**
   - The code has no second copy: 433 commits live only on this laptop.
   - There are no crash reports.
   - One bad event can stall a player's sync for good.
   - Nothing stops abuse of a database the wedding site shares.

**Finishing it** is about five to eight weeks of focused work in five phases (section 5). The order matters:

1. Safety nets first.
2. Then a real pilot with friends, to measure whether people come back on day 7 and day 30.
3. Then depth for the second month (more to unlock, your own quests, editing the profile).
4. Then one or two bigger bets (friends' parties, steps from Health Connect, app blocking).
5. Last, the hardening any public release needs.

## 1. How useful and valuable is it?

**Who it serves best:** beginners to self-improvement aged roughly 16–30 who like anime and manhwa. People who want
structure but have quit stricter apps. People with a limitation (knee, back, shoulder, wrist), or starting from zero.
The anime niche ignores that last group completely: one competitor's reviewers say it expects a rank-E beginner to do
pull-ups and has no filter for limitations or equipment.

**The job it does:** it decides what to do today (a core quest, two side quests and a short article) and keeps it small
enough to finish. It guides each quest with steps, safety lines, a timer, a rep log and a rest timer. It records
progress the player can see: XP, levels, five stats with ranks, a portrait that evolves, and a calendar.

**How useful that is, judged on the design:** high for the first three to four weeks. The day builder is deterministic
and personal. Difficulty rises only when the player shows they're ready. "I felt pain", hard days, rests and swaps mean
there's always a doable version of the day. The kind check-in after a miss matches what the research on streaks
recommends: let people repair a streak, and don't make them blame themselves. Punishing apps lose people. A Habitica
field study found that every participant ran into its punishments backfiring.

**How valuable it is, judged on evidence:** unknown. The live pilot report (2026-10-15, read only):

| | |
|---|---|
| Characters | 3, all made in the last 7 days, all Warriors; one is Vera, the emulator's test character |
| Logged in the last 7 days | 2 |
| Furthest progress | 4 quests cleared over 2 days, level 2 |
| Check-ins answered, rests, hard days, pain reports | none yet |

Mage, Rogue and Monk have never been played, and the pacing has only been checked by simulation. Typical Health &
Fitness apps keep about 3–8 of every 100 installs after 30 days (*unverified* aggregator figures). Whether LevelMeUp
beats that is the single most important unknown.

## 2. Strengths

**Product**

- **Safe exercise built in.**
  - The PAR-Q safety answers are never stored.
  - A reported limitation closes the risky rungs.
  - Pain never costs XP, no set goes to failure, and a hard day still counts.
  - No competitor found does this.
- **Fair failure.**
  - A protected miss pauses the streak instead of breaking it.
  - Honesty plus the recovery quest earns Discipline.
  - Outages are protected automatically, and a lighter plan is offered, never forced.
- **Accountability with consent.** The Shadow is off by default, previewed in full and turned off with one tap. It
  appears at most once a day, pauses after three in seven days, and is never triggered by a technical failure. A lint
  bans shaming words in the copy.
- **A daily cited article with a takeaway quest.** Nobody in the niche has this. The library is shared, so the cost is
  flat (about $0.25 an article, capped at $25 a month).
- **Look and feel.** A consistent Ink Panel style: a portrait for each class, sex and tier, plus the Shadow (25 in all),
  33 quest pictures, motion, haptics and a widget. The app looks finished on the emulator.
- **Works offline**, with a personal day boundary that handles time zones and DST.
- **No ads, no trackers, no paywall.** Deletion has been checked end to end.

**Engineering**

- **The server decides every reward.** The phone never sends an XP amount. Each quest pays once, inside its own day;
  reused events are refused; amounts are clamped.
- **Row-level security on all 15 tables**, with tests that walk every table. The project's own `authenticated` role is
  locked out of the `levelmeup` schema. No secrets are in the app, and a bundle scan checks that on every build.
- **One rulebook runs on both phone and server** (`packages/rules`), so numbers are instant on the phone and refereed
  on the server.
- **3,017 tests pass**: app 942, content 1,113, rules 450, server 512. Every workspace typechecks clean.
- **Fails safely.** An outbox that is safe to resend, backoff, a minimum app version, an outage treated as an outage
  and never as a sign-out, and a template reveal when Claude is down.

## 3. Weaknesses

### Product

1. **The second month is empty.**
   - The last ability and the last portrait tier come at level 10, about 17–25 days for a steady player.
   - Ladders have 5–6 rungs and step up at most once a week, so most top out after 5–8 weeks: push-ups at 15
     minutes, walking at 30, reading at 35.
   - After that, only the remaining achievements are left. This is where day-30 retention is won or lost, and it is
     the same "value fades" problem Life Reset has.
2. **Little variety.** 80 quest templates, but many share a name: "Read" ×6, "Phone-free block" ×5, "Walk" ×4. The
   three voices reword the same quests.
3. **Notifications are effectively absent.** They are off until the player opens Settings, and onboarding never offers
   them. The daily loop depends on remembering to open the app.
4. **Rigid.**
   - Players can't add their own habits. That is the most common feature in competing apps and the most requested.
   - Name, goals and limitations can't be changed after onboarding.
   - **"Gentle start" is permanent.** No route back to strength work exists, even after a doctor clears the
     player. That is both a safety and a fairness dead end.
5. **A lot to learn.** Ranks, shields, recovery quests, a five-answer check-in, three Pact levels, the seal, eight
   abilities, swaps, hard days, rests, pain and the lighter plan. The 0.5.0 "Clarity" pass helped, but day one is
   still dense.
6. **Solo and narrow.** No friends or party, English only, Android only, an APK installed by hand. A guest who never
   saves with Google loses the character with the phone.
7. **The original edge isn't built.** Blocking apps until the quests are done was the idea against Life Reset. Today's
   level-2 "seal" lives only on the phone.
8. **Hardly verified on a real phone.**
   - Only the M4 rows have Find X9 results.
   - These flows have never run end to end on any device:
     - a real workout day;
     - most of the check-in;
     - a real Shadow miss;
     - Stillness, Dawn Ritual and Vanish;
     - Insight;
     - a level-up portrait in real play.
9. **The name is hard to find.** Google Play already lists "Level Up Life", "LevelUp.Do", "Level UP: Fitness", "Level
   Up: Solo Leveling System" and others.

### Engineering

| Severity | Finding |
|---|---|
| **High** | **One copy of the source.** No git remote and no CI: 433 commits exist only on this laptop. |
| **High** for any public release, low for a small pilot | **No abuse controls.** No rate limit on `lmu-sync`, about 90 database round trips per call, and the database is a Micro instance shared with the wedding. No bot check on guest sign-ups. Architecture §8 promises a rate limit that was never built. |
| **Medium** | **A stuck event stalls a player for good.** An unexpected server error fails the whole sync with a 500 (`supabase/functions/lmu-sync/index.ts:318-321`). The phone resends the same event forever and nothing gets past it. |
| **Medium** | **No crash reports, no error boundary, no alerts.** A crash in a release build closes the app silently. |
| **Medium** | **One $25 Claude budget covers both reveals and articles.** Scripted guests could spend it. There is no spend limit in the Anthropic Console. |
| **Medium** | **The shared wedding project widens the blast radius.** Its service-role key is in every edge function. Its Auth settings and guest clean-ups apply to LevelMeUp's guests. |
| **Medium** | **Articles publish nightly without a person reading them.** The code checks are good but don't check facts. Health content from web search can be wrong. |
| **Medium** | **No staging.** Migrations and the live tests run first on production. `lmu-sync`, `db.ts` and the onboarding and delete functions have no offline tests. |
| **Low–Medium** | **Accessibility caps.** Text stops growing at 1.5× (body) and 1.15× (titles), where Android allows 2×. TalkBack itself has never been driven on the phone. A reduced-motion change needs a restart. |
| **Low** | **Maintainability.** `state/store.ts` is 754 lines doing everything. `levelmeup.adjust` is redefined six times across migrations. Some docs have drifted (the art version, the rate limit). |
| **Low** | **A rare blank core quest card.** Seen once this session on the emulator: Today's core quest card stayed invisible because its slam-in animation stuck at its first frame. It was the development build, on its first launch right after the emulator booted. Six cold starts of the 0.5.0 release were all fine. A safety net is cheap. |

## 4. Potential

**The niche is crowded but shallow.** About 25 "System" apps on Play. Most have under 10,000 installs and the biggest
have about 500,000. Their reviews complain about ads, wiped progress, punishing rules and one-size workouts. Kind,
reliable and safe is a real opening there, but it doesn't show on a store page. It has to be the pitch: *"The System
won't kill you on day 1, unless you sign the Pact."*

**Audience:**

- Anime and manhwa fans worldwide. Solo Leveling stays popular, and a third season is expected around 2027–28
  (*unverified*).
- Exercise beginners and people with limitations.
- The Philippines in particular: English works, about 90% of phones run Android, and about 35% of people watch anime
  (*unverified*).

**A realistic first year** with no marketing budget: 1,000–20,000 installs (an estimate). 100,000 or more would need a
viral moment.

**Money that fits the app's values:**

- A free core forever.
- A one-time **Supporter unlock** (about $4.99, about ₱149–199 in the Philippines) for cosmetics: extra portrait
  tiers, Shadow skins, themes.
- A "can't afford it" option like Finch's.
- **Avoid:** ads, auto-charging trials, and putting the articles behind a subscription.

Monthly costs are the $25 Claude cap, plus about $10 if LevelMeUp moves to its own Supabase project (*unverified*:
check the dashboard's price). At $4.99, after Google's 15%, that is roughly 9 sales a month.

**What would raise its value most**, in order:

1. Proof of retention.
2. A second-month arc.
3. Your own quests.
4. Small parties.
5. Steps from Health Connect.
6. A distinctive name.

## 5. What it takes to finish the final version (Play Store excluded)

Sizes: **S** about a day, **M** a few days, **L** a week or more. Every phase ends with tests, the emulator check and,
where it matters, the Find X9.

### Phase 0 — Safety nets (about 1 week)

| # | Work | Size | Needs your yes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | A private copy of the code (a private GitHub repo) and CI: tests, typecheck, the portable and bundle checks on every push | S | Yes (outward; free) |
| 0.2 | Crash and error visibility: an error boundary that shows a calm "something broke" screen and logs it; optionally Sentry's free tier | S–M | Only for Sentry |
| 0.3 | A stuck event can't block sync: unexpected per-event errors become a refusal with a code; the phone sets an event aside after N tries | S | Deploy |
| 0.4 | The server refuses "rest today" on a day that already counts (carried from M10c) | S | Deploy |
| 0.5 | The core quest card can never stay invisible (a safety net in `SlamIn`) | S | — |
| 0.6 | The 0.5.1 preview APK with the "Add a set" fix and the above | S | EAS build |

### Phase 1 — A real pilot (2–3 weeks, mostly waiting)

| # | Work | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 8–15 friends as Google test users, playing for at least 14 days (a 14-day closed test is also what Play will ask for later) | S (your part: the Gmails) |
| 1.2 | Offer notifications during onboarding, still opt-in: one screen, "Want a morning briefing and an evening nudge?" | S–M |
| 1.3 | Retention in `npm run pilot`: day-1, day-7 and day-30 from the server's own completions, with no tracking SDK | S |
| 1.4 | A simple feedback route: a "Send feedback" row that opens an email or a form | S |
| 1.5 | Finish the Find X9 checklist, and run the never-run flows on the emulator with a test character: a workout day, the check-in, a Shadow miss, the abilities | M |

### Phase 2 — The second month (2–3 weeks)

| # | Work | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Growth past level 10.** Portrait tiers at 15, 20 and 30; a new ability or upgrade per class; titles. Ladders extended or "mastery" rungs at the top | L (art plus rules) |
| 2.2 | **A weekly Gate.** A boss fed by the week's completions, beaten or not, with no penalty for losing; a Gate record in the Chronicle | M–L |
| 2.3 | **Your own quests.** 1–3 personal habits, check-off only, outside the safety ladders, with capped XP | M |
| 2.4 | **Edit the profile.** Name, goals and limitations in Settings; a way out of "gentle start" that re-asks the safety check | M |
| 2.5 | **Variety.** Two or three alternatives per rung so a week doesn't repeat; more article topic seeds | M |
| 2.6 | **A gentler day one.** Show abilities, the Pact, shields and the lighter plan only when they first matter | M |

### Phase 3 — One or two bigger bets (choose, 1–3 weeks each)

- **A party:** 2–4 friends see each other's day cleared and send a nudge; no public leaderboard. Habitica found small
  parties thrived while its public spaces faded. **L**
- **Health Connect steps:** a walking quest verified by the phone's step count (MAINARC already has this code). **M**
- **App blocking:** the original differentiator. A feasibility spike first (Android usage access on the Find X9). **L**,
  and risky.

### Phase 4 — Hardening for any public release (1–2 weeks)

| # | Work | Size | Needs your yes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Rate limits in `lmu-sync` and `lmu-onboarding`; a bot check (Turnstile) on guest sign-up, or require Google; a separate reveal budget; a spend limit in the Anthropic Console | M | Deploy; the Console is yours |
| 4.2 | **The backend decision:** move LevelMeUp to its own Supabase project, with a one-time guest transfer, or stay until the wedding site retires | L | Yes (monthly cost) |
| 4.3 | A staging project, or at least offline tests for `lmu-sync` and the database layer | M | Cost if a project |
| 4.4 | Articles: a person approves before publishing (or a source-quality filter), plus a "report this article" button | M | — |
| 4.5 | A hosted privacy policy and a web page to delete a character | M | Hosting |
| 4.6 | Accessibility: text caps up toward 2×, a real TalkBack run on the Find X9, reduced motion without a restart | M | — |
| 4.7 | A release build profile (AAB, shrinking) and a recorded app size | S–M | EAS |
| 4.8 | Tidy-up: split `store.ts`, a schema snapshot after each migration, remove `recentDays`, fix the drifted docs | M | — |
| 4.9 | Name and pitch: a distinctive name, with no "Solo Leveling" in it | S | Yours |

## 6. Decisions for the owner

1. **Back up the code to a private GitHub repo now?** *Recommended: yes.* It's free, and today a dead laptop loses
   everything.
2. **Run the real pilot before building more?** *Recommended: yes, Phase 0 then Phase 1.* Send the friends' Gmails.
   Retention numbers decide whether Phase 2 or Phase 3 matters more.
3. **The backend:** *Recommended: stay on the shared project for a pilot under about 20 people, and decide on moving
   before any public release.* Moving costs about $10 a month (*unverified*) and a guest transfer, and it gets harder
   as players arrive.
4. **The Phase 3 bet:** *Recommended: Health Connect steps first (small, reuses MAINARC's code), then a party if the
   pilot shows people want company.*
5. **Monetization:** *Recommended: decide the Supporter unlock before the public release.* No subscription until there
   are retention numbers.
